Science criteria — MYP Sciences

Every m10 paper is written against the four Sciences criteria. The examples below are real questions taken from ESS Unit A Part 1 and Part 2, so each criterion is shown with the kind of task that actually earns marks against it.

CriterionWhat it rewardsExample from this library
A
Knowing &
Understanding		 Accurate scientific content, precise terminology, correct classification and definitions. No loose language. Part 1 · Biodiversity — “Under the biological species concept, two organisms belong to the same species when they…”
Part 1 · Origin of Life — “LUCA, the Last Universal Common Ancestor, is best described as…”
Part 1 · Origin of Life — “The Alvarez team concluded that an asteroid impact caused the end-Cretaceous extinction. Which piece of evidence was the decisive clue?”
B
Inquiring		 Framing a question, choosing and justifying a method, planning fieldwork, handling variables. Part 1 · Populations — quadrats, transects and capture–mark–recapture: choosing the method that suits the organism and its mobility, and justifying the choice.
Part 1 · Populations — “Scientists estimate there are between 8 and 20 million species of eukaryote on Earth… Explain THREE reasons why these estimates vary so widely.”
C
Processing &
Evaluating		 Processing data, interpreting trends, evaluating method and limitations, drawing a supported conclusion. Part 2 · Galápagos — students are given mean and standard deviation of ground finch beak depth, then asked: “Which chart is the most appropriate for showing the frequency distribution?” and “A population of finches has a very large standard deviation in beak depth. What does this most likely indicate?”
Part 2 · Evolution — “Evaluate the limitations of the fossil record as a source of evidence about evolution. Give at least FOUR limitations and, for each, explain how it affects our conclusions.”
D
Communicating		 Accurate, purposeful, well-structured communication of scientific argument, with correct vocabulary and a clear line of reasoning. Part 2 · Evolution — extended response: “Explain how homologous structures provide evidence that groups of organisms share a common ancestor. Use the pentadactyl limb as your example.”
Part 2 — structured essay: “Apply the ideas of divergent and convergent evolution to real organisms… a) Define both terms, stating which type of structure each one produces. (2 marks)” — marks awarded per identified element, not per paragraph.
The MCQ items still test A and C

Part 1 runs 15 MCQ + 14 short; Part 2 runs 12 MCQ + 15 short. An MCQ is not automatically Criterion A — “Which chart is most appropriate for showing the frequency distribution?” is a Criterion C judgement dressed as a multiple choice. The command term decides the criterion, not the format.

Individuals & Society criteria

Four criteria, weighted equally at 25% each. Both live I&S papers — History & Future of Money and Supply & Demand — are built on these.

CriterionWhat it rewardsExample from this library
A
Knowing &
Understanding		 Accurate content and correct economic or historical terminology. Money — “What was the critical innovation of Lydian coinage around 600 BCE?”
Money — “What event in 1971 marked the end of the Bretton Woods system?”
Supply & Demand — “Which of the following best describes the Law of Demand?”
B
Investigating		 Framing an inquiry, choosing a method, gathering and organising information. Supply & Demand — case-study work built on live events: the Strait of Hormuz closure cutting 20% of global oil supply, or the DRC cobalt crisis reducing supply by 40% with knock-on effects for Volvo and Polestar EV batteries. Students identify which curve shifts and in which direction, then defend the choice.
C
Communicating		 Accurate, purposeful, structured communication — including correct citation practice. Money — “In MLA format, how would you cite a YouTube video by…”, testing that a student can attribute a source properly rather than only recall its content.
Supply & Demand — “Calculate the percentage change in purchasing power… Show your working and state whether Person A has gained or lost.”
D
Thinking Critically		 Judgement, weighing sources, evaluating a position — the heaviest criterion in both papers. Money — “Evaluate whether Bitcoin can function as…” a unit of account, medium of exchange, store of value; and “A major criticism of Proof-of-Work cryptocurrency is…” Framed with OPVL — Origin, Purpose, Value, Limitation — and PESTEL — Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal.
Where the frameworks appear

OPVL is the source-analysis workhorse for the Money paper — a currency, a coin or a blockchain is examined for who issued it, what it was for, what it was worth, and what limits its use. PESTEL handles the contextual question of why a monetary or market development happened at all, and the Money paper tests it directly: “In the PESTEL model, …”

Command terms, and why they decide the criterion

The platform's standard forbids letting an explain be answered with a bare state. The command term is what assigns a question to a criterion and sets the mark count.

Command termWhat the answer must containTypical marks
State · Define · OutlineOne fact, or a precise definition. No causal language required.1–2
DescribeFeatures or characteristics, with the required quantity stated in the stem.3–4
ExplainA cause-and-effect chain. Every link must be present, or the mark is not awarded.4–6
Compare · DistinguishBoth sides treated explicitly. Naming a pair without contrasting them scores nothing.4–7
JustifyArgument plus evidence plus reasoning. The evidence alone is not enough.6–9
EvaluateBalanced judgement on both sides, with a stated conclusion. Always the last block in a paper.8–12
Diagnostic distractors are the Criterion A test

In an MCQ, the wrong options are not filler. Each one maps to a named misconception, and at least one question per paper forces a choice between a confusable pair under conditions that make the distinction load-bearing. “Which is the best example of divergent evolution?” fails a student who has not separated divergent from convergent — the distractor did the teaching.

How Tests Are Built

The authoring standard every new paper is measured against. It is written down, and it is executable — not aspirational.

1 · The standard

High IB standard

  • Answer the question that is asked — no padding, no restating the stem
  • Exact IB terminology; loose student language is addressed in the scheme
  • Every mark defensible from the mark scheme alone
  • Command terms realised properly — state ≠ explain ≠ justify ≠ evaluate
  • No recall-only trivia in a timed paper
  • Difficulty ramps within a paper: recall → application → analysis → evaluation

Competitive-exam alignment

  • Format anchored to IB MYP Sciences Paper 1 (short structured) and Paper 2 (extended, data-based)
  • Enriched toward IB Science Olympiad / IChO-style data interrogation
  • Also draws on UKMT, BBO, USABO problem styles
  • Data must be interrogated — trend, magnitude, method, limitation — not read off
  • Citation honesty is non-negotiable. Never invent a paper number, year or statistic. A fabricated citation is worse than none.
  • Estimates are labelled as estimates, with source and uncertainty

2 · Planning before writing

The topic breakdown and the differentiation list are written before any question:

  • Which confusable pairs the paper will test
  • Which command terms, and in what ramp order
  • Which data set students will interrogate, and its stated limitation
  • Marks and time allocation — MCQ 1 mark, short 2–7, extended 8–12; roughly 1.5–2 minutes per mark

3 · The question schema

TypeRequired fieldsMarks
mcqid · text · type · marks · options[] · correct (index) · explanation · topic1
shortas above plus hint · keywords[] · scheme[]2–7
extendedas above8–12
Field discipline

options and correct are the raw source of truth. The template derives _opts / _correct through a deterministic seeded shuffle (seed id × 97 + 41). Display order is never hand-edited.

4 · Marking written answers

How it scores

One distinct keyword hit = one mark, capped at the marks available. This is presented in the UI as a self-check estimate, never as a marker's decision.

Proportional scoring is forbidden — with realistic pools of 30–90 keywords it makes full marks unreachable and reports a meaningless score.

What every paper ships with

  • Countdown timer with warnings
  • Question navigator and progress bar
  • Mark scheme modal
  • Per-answer review
  • Per-topic score breakdown
  • Deterministic option shuffle — no letter is ever reliably the answer
  • jsPDF export
  • localStorage history

5 · Study page standard

  • Leads with the exam task, not the textbook — what the student must be able to do
  • Worked examples with reasoning shown, not just answers
  • Carries the actual data the papers use; simplified figures are labelled
  • Ends sections with self-check questions that discriminate the confusable pairs
  • Glossary of examinable terms — doubles as the keyword source for the papers
  • Swedish context used where it genuinely helps, never forced
Local context, where honest

Volvo · H&M · Northvolt · Oatly · Spotify · Riksbank · Nord Pool · ICA · Baltic Sea Gadus morhua stock collapse · Ecodiscus roeseli · Öland's alvar grasslands · Betula pendula postglacial recolonisation · MRSA in Swedish hospitals. A fabricated local statistic is a defect.

Paper Blueprint

How a single paper is laid out, from the current-template reference shape.

Question ramp

  • Block 1 — recall & define. Short answers, single-idea. Establishes the terminology boundary.
  • Block 2 — apply. Straightforward use of a formula or model in an unfamiliar setting.
  • Block 3 — analyse. Multi-step; the data has to be interrogated rather than read.
  • Block 4 — evaluate. Balanced judgement on both sides, with a conclusion. Always last.

A flat paper wastes the grade range. Every mark must be defensible on its own.

Time & mark budget

  • MCQ — 1 mark, ~1.5 min
  • Short — 2–7 marks, ~1.5–2 min per mark
  • Extended — 8–12 marks, ~2 min per mark

MCQ construction

  • Options never duplicated
  • correct must be an integer inside the option range
  • No two defensible options
  • No option correct "for a different reason" than intended
  • Fewer than 6 options is preferred

Confusion Pairs

Students lose marks on pairs of easily confused ideas, not on isolated facts. Differentiation is a first-class requirement, not a stylistic extra.

In study material

  • Every section containing a confusable pair gets an explicit contrast table or a "Do not confuse" callout
  • State why students confuse them — the misconception is the teaching point
  • End each section with a self-check that forces the distinction
  • Distractors must be diagnostic, not decorative — each wrong option maps to a named misconception
  • A student who picks it should be able to say what they got wrong
  • At least one question per paper forces a choice between the two ideas under a scenario that makes the distinction load-bearing

Quality Gate

Content is validated, not eyeballed. npm run validate must exit 0 before anything is reported as done.

Blocking errors

CheckRule
Inline script syntaxMust parse (vm.Script)
Question schemaEvery question has id, text, type, marks; ids unique
MCQ rangecorrect is an integer within options
MCQ optionsNo duplicates
Mark scheme vs marksmarks >= scheme.length — never list more scheme points than marks available
Keyword coveragekeywords.length >= marks — otherwise full marks is unreachable
Answer-key biasNo single letter may win more than 50% of the MCQ
HTML balanceOpening vs closing block tags must match

Warnings only

Missing topic label · absent seededShuffle() · lopsided key · more than 5 MCQ options · MCQ carrying a mark scheme.

Then verify the build

  • npm run validate — all files in assessments/
  • node scripts/validate-assessment.mjs assessments/<file>.html — one file
  • npm run build — must produce dist/assessments/<file>.html

Only current-template files — those with both showMarkScheme and seededShuffle — are gated. The 15 legacy myp4-* files are reported, not gated.
