Description

*Conceptual Understanding
In this unit we are exploring how principles and discoveries help shape models through adaptation to the context. We see how the form of a quadratic equation influences what patterns we see and how, while there is equivalence between different forms of equations, they are differently suitable to different questions. The shape of the parabola invites studetns to ask questions about change, contrasting the changing gradient of a parabola with the constant gradient of a linear function. The parabola represents an idealized model of movement through the air, which is in contrast both to Aristotle's views from Ancient times, but also to the observations made when air resistance is non-negligible. This formed part of the discovery of modern mechanics and will prompt reflection on what makes a good model,in different contexts.

*Statement of Inquiry
Quadratic equations are important models in many situations, and can be written in different forms. These forms, while equivalent, show different patterns and describe the change in variables in different ways. Adaptation of a model to suit a particular problem enables discoveries such as the principles of motion. It was also historically a driving force behind the need to rigorously understand how infinitesimal Changes can be described mathematically.
